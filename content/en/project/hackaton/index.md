The **Exoplanet Hackathon** is an innovative educational framework based on Challenge-Based Learning (CBL). It was developed as my undergraduate thesis project, receiving the highest distinction (Meritorious) to obtain my BA degree in Natural Sciences Education from the **Facultad de Educación at Universidad de La Sabana** (Colombia).

### 💡 The Pedagogical Approach

The hackathon was designed as an intensive, active-learning strategy where students tackle real-world astrophysics challenges, built upon three pillars:
* **Challenge-Based Learning (CBL)**: Participants solve open-ended problems inspired by modern exoplanetary research (such as classifying true transit candidates versus astrophysical false positives using actual satellite datasets).
* **STEM Integration**: Synthesizes core concepts of stellar physics, mathematical modeling, and programming/software tools in a highly engaging, collaborative environment.
* **Development of Inquiry Skills**: Evaluates and measures students' growth in fundamental research practices: formulating testable hypotheses, processing quantitative stellar flux profiles, collaborating within diverse teams, and communicating findings.

### 🏆 Impact and Distinctions

* **Meritorious Thesis Award**: Officially recognized with honors by the academic board for its pedagogical rigor, innovative assessment tools, and scientific validity.
* **Active Science Education**: Served as a blueprint for active-learning astronomy workshops and educational activities aimed at democratizing planetary and space sciences in both rural and urban high schools across Colombia.
